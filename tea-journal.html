03 · In Conversation

A tea with Mr. Iyer.

Interview · 22 min read

Ravi Iyer

Third-generation tea taster

Interviewed at his kitchen table in Munnar, over the seventh cup of the morning.
Words by Nadia Kapoor

Q

What did your father teach you that nobody else could?

Patience, mostly. He would sit with a single leaf for ten minutes before he tasted it. Not because the tasting took ten minutes — because he wanted to look at it that long. I still do that with the first batch of every season.

"A good cup of chai is never in a hurry. Neither is the person drinking it."
Q

Does the origin of the leaf really matter that much?

It matters the way the soil matters in a wine. Two gardens forty kilometres apart will taste completely different with the same processing. That is why we visit every garden we buy from — at least once every two years.

Q

And the one thing you would never do in a chai?

Boil the milk twice. I have seen it done and it is a small crime. Warm it once, slowly, and take it off the heat before it thinks about bubbling over.

Q

What are you drinking right now?

A second-flush Nilgiri, no milk, one small piece of jaggery. It is what I drink when I want to remember why I chose this work at all.
